# Proposal: My Daily Time Spending

Originally written for the proposal stage on 30 October 2026, updated on 23 November 2026 to match the current state of the project. Final submission: 2 December 2026.

## Project description and aim

This project explores how my recorded time is distributed between work, study, sport, leisure, social activities, meetings and trips. The aim is to understand which activities take the most time and how my days and weeks differ.

The dataset is [`data/time_spending.csv`](data/time_spending.csv): 33 activity records on 20 consecutive days (2026-10-19 to 2026-11-07), 176 recorded hours, five columns (`date`, `activity`, `category`, `duration_hours`, `notes`). Each row is one activity I recorded by hand. It is a partial log of the main activities, not a complete account of every hour.

## Research questions

1. Where does my recorded time go: which categories take the largest share of the hours?
2. How different are Work and Study: in total hours and in the length of a typical session?
3. Do I work and study on the same days, or do they exclude each other?
4. Do my weeks look alike, or does the balance between Work and Study change from week to week?
5. How do weekends differ from weekdays: in the amount of recorded time and in what fills it?
6. Which days were the most and the least loaded, and what explains the extremes?

The answers are in the Findings section of the [README](README.md). The weekend result rests on only 5 days and the last week has only 6 recorded days, so those comparisons are tentative.

## Data and measurement

All values are recorded by me; no data was downloaded, exported or generated by AI. The columns, units and validation rules are in the [data dictionary](Data_Dictionary.md).

Recording rules: one row per activity, recorded on the day it happens, duration in hours. The CSV does not contain start and end times, so the exact measurement method, rounding and any overlap between activities are described in the diary section of the README. Known case: on 2026-10-24 a Competition and a Trip were recorded on the same day (14 h in total).

## The application

The application is written in Python and run from `src/main.py`:

- `src/db.py` validates the CSV and loads it into an SQLite table.
- `src/queries.py` holds six SQL queries (`GROUP BY`, `ORDER BY`, `WHERE`, `COUNT`, `SUM`, `AVG`).
- `src/analysis.py` uses pandas for the analysis (category totals, weekday analysis, weekday vs weekend, work vs study, correlation) and matplotlib for six charts saved in `charts/`.
- `src/main.py` is a text menu to filter and view entries, run the SQL queries, see the analysis, make charts and add a new entry (validated, saved to the CSV and the database).
